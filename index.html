Neil Gautam

I work on robots that learn from people, and anticipate what they'll do next.

My research centers on understanding people in 3D, so robots can learn from them and work safely around them. At Berkshire Grey, I build the data-collection cells that turn human demonstrations into training data for robot foundation models on industrial arms. With Prof. Cheng Zhang at Texas A&M, I work on egocentric human motion prediction: forecasting how a person's whole body will move toward a goal, from a head-mounted camera that can barely see it. I work across the full stack, from motion capture and camera calibration to generative models to production deployment, backed by two years shipping ML at AfterShoot, Savart and HyperVerge.

Graduating December 2026. Open to full-time robot learning and ML research roles.

Research

M.S. Thesis · May 2025 – present

Foresight to Motion: goal-conditioned autoregressive human motion prediction from egocentric video

Predicts full-body human trajectories from a single egocentric RGB frame, conditioned on a goal point or on running video, with no depth, point clouds or camera extrinsics. Predicted SMPL sequences are validated in NVIDIA ProtoMotions3 and published through a ROS 2 action interface.

  1. Egocentric RGB
  2. Goal point
  3. Autoregressive diffusion
  4. SMPL motion
  5. ProtoMotions3 · ROS 2

Advisor: Prof. Cheng Zhang · Submitted to ICLR 2027

Jan 2025 – May 2025

HG-SCRUB: human Gaussian splatting with scene relighting & unbaking

A 3DGS framework for joint human–scene decomposition that learns physically based materials, using depth-derived normal gradients and Gaussian baking for indirect illumination.

Advisor: Prof. Wenping Wang

Project page

Aug 2024 – Dec 2024

Free-Generation: training-free trajectory control in video diffusion

Found a domain shift in attention-block activation statistics of video LDMs and proposed MaskNorm, a zero-parameter normalization layer that gives correct spatio-temporal trajectory control on DAVIS-16 without training.
